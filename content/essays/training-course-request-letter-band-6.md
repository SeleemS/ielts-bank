## Prompt
You would like to attend a training course that would help you do your job better, but you need your employer's approval and support to take part. Write a letter to your manager.

In your letter:

- describe the training course you would like to attend
- explain how it would help you in your current role
- say what support you are asking your manager to provide

Write at least 150 words. You do NOT need to write any addresses.

Begin your letter as follows: Dear Mr Anderson,

## Essay
Dear Mr Anderson,

I am writing to ==ask your permission to attend== a training course. The course is called Data Analysis with Excel and it is organise by a training centre in the city centre. It is three days course, from 12 to 14 November, and it teach how to use formulas, charts and ==pivot tables== for analyse data.

As you know, in my job I prepare ==the monthly sales report== for our departement. At the moment I do many things by hand and it take a lot of time. If I do this course, I can do the report more quick and with less mistakes. Also I can help my collegues when they have problem with Excel, so it is good for all the team.

I would like to ask you if the company can ==pay the fee==, which is 450 pounds. I also need three days free from work. It would be really great if you say yes!

I hope you will agree with my request. ==Thank you very much for your time.==

Kind regards,

Marta

## Task Achievement
All three bullet points are addressed, and the purpose is clear from the opening sentence. The course is described with useful facts (name, dates, content), and the second bullet gives a relevant benefit linked to the writer's actual job. However, the support requested is covered only briefly — the fee and time off are stated, but there is no suggestion of how her work would be covered while she is away, which a manager would expect. The tone is mostly polite and semi-formal, but it slips with "It would be really great if you say yes!", which sounds too casual for a request to a manager. Adequate but uneven coverage with an inconsistent tone is Band 6.

## Coherence and Cohesion
The letter is clearly organised, with one paragraph for each bullet point and a short closing, so there is a clear overall progression. Within paragraphs, however, the linking is basic and mechanical: sentences are joined mainly with "and", "so" and "Also", and the second paragraph is a series of short statements rather than a connected argument. Referencing is sometimes unclear — "it take a lot of time" does not make clear whether "it" is the report or the manual work. This is typical Band 6 cohesion.

## Lexical Resource
Lexical Resource is the weakest criterion. There is some relevant work vocabulary ("pivot tables", "the monthly sales report", "pay the fee"), but the range is limited and "do" is over-used ("I do many things", "If I do this course", "do the report"). Errors in word form and spelling are frequent: "for analyse data" (*for analysing data*), "more quick" (*more quickly*), "less mistakes" (*fewer mistakes*), "departement" (*department*) and "collegues" (*colleagues*). The collocation "three days free from work" should be *three days' leave*. Individually these do not block meaning, but together they make the reader work harder, which places vocabulary at Band 5.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy
There is a mix of simple and complex forms, including a conditional ("If I do this course, I can…") and a non-defining relative clause ("which is 450 pounds"). There are, however, several errors: the passive is incorrectly formed in "it is organise" (*it is organised*), the article is missing in "It is three days course" (*a three-day course*) and "when they have problem", and there are agreement errors in "it teach" and "it take". These errors are noticeable but rarely reduce communication, so grammar is rated Band 6.

## Vocabulary
- **ask your permission to attend** — correct and polite; at Band 7+ try *request your approval to attend*.
- **pivot tables** — precise technical term that shows the writer knows the course content.
- **the monthly sales report** — specific and relevant; it connects the course to the current role.
- **pay the fee** — correct collocation; a more formal upgrade is *cover the course fee*.
- **Thank you very much for your time.** — a polite, appropriate closing line for a request to a manager.

## Next band
- Replace the casual "It would be really great if you say yes!" with a polite, consistent request such as *I would be very grateful for your support.*
- Extend the third bullet: offer to arrange cover or to finish urgent tasks before the course, so the manager can see you have thought about the team.
- Vary your verbs instead of repeating "do" (*complete the report*, *take the course*, *carry out tasks manually*) and check the spelling of workplace words such as *department* and *colleagues*.
