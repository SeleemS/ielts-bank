## Prompt
A growing number of students choose to leave their home country in order to complete a university degree in another part of the world.

Do the advantages of studying for a degree abroad outweigh the disadvantages?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

## Essay
Each year, a growing number of young people cross borders to earn their degrees, often at considerable expense. Although this choice carries genuine financial and emotional risks, I believe that for most students the benefits of studying abroad substantially outweigh the drawbacks.

The most tangible advantage is access to education that may be unavailable at home. Certain fields, such as specialised engineering or medical research, are concentrated in a small number of universities, and studying at one of them gives students access to ==state-of-the-art facilities== and to academics who lead their disciplines. A qualification from an internationally recognised institution can also ==open doors== with employers who might otherwise overlook a candidate. Just as valuable is the linguistic and cultural fluency that comes from living in another society: a graduate who has written a dissertation in a second language and worked in multinational teams is ==well equipped== for an increasingly globalised labour market.

Beyond these professional gains, studying abroad accelerates personal development. Removed from familiar support systems, students must manage their own budgets, navigate unfamiliar bureaucracy and ==forge friendships== across cultural boundaries. Such experiences tend to produce graduates who are more self-reliant and more tolerant of difference, qualities that are difficult to acquire in a lecture hall.

The disadvantages, however, are real. International tuition fees and living costs can place a heavy strain on family finances, and not every student adjusts well: isolation and ==culture shock== can undermine both wellbeing and academic results, particularly in the first year. From a national perspective, sending countries may also suffer a ==brain drain== when their most able graduates choose to settle overseas. Yet most of these drawbacks can be mitigated. Scholarships and part-time work reduces the financial burden, universities increasingly offer dedicated support for international students, and graduates who remain abroad often contribute to their home countries through investment and research partnerships.

In short, studying for a degree abroad is a demanding undertaking, but the academic opportunities, career advantages and personal growth it offers make it a worthwhile investment for most students who are able to prepare for it properly.

## Task Response
The question is sufficiently addressed and the position — that the benefits "substantially outweigh the drawbacks" for most students — is clear and consistent. Ideas are relevant, extended and supported: academic access is illustrated with specialised fields, employability is linked to specific experiences (a dissertation in a second language, multinational teams), and the disadvantages are not only described but weighed, with the essay showing how each can be reduced. The qualification "for most students who are able to prepare for it properly" keeps the argument honest. For Band 9 the writer could treat the brain-drain issue from the home country's side in more depth rather than answering it quite briefly.

## Coherence and Cohesion
The essay sequences information logically, moving from academic to professional to personal benefits and then to a disadvantages paragraph that turns into a rebuttal ("Yet most of these drawbacks can be mitigated"). Cohesion is managed well through precise reference ("one of them", "Such experiences", "these drawbacks") and through transitions that grow out of the previous idea ("Just as valuable is…", "Beyond these professional gains"). Paragraphing is sufficient and appropriate. Only the slightly signposted "however" and "In short" separate this from the near-invisible cohesion of Band 9.

## Lexical Resource
A wide range of vocabulary is used fluently and flexibly to express precise meanings: "tangible advantage", "navigate unfamiliar bureaucracy", "tolerant of difference", "place a heavy strain on" and "mitigated" are all accurate and natural. Less common items are handled skilfully, including the idiomatic "open doors" and the precise contrast between "sending countries" and graduates who "settle overseas". There are no errors in word choice, spelling or word formation, which supports a secure Band 8.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy
A wide range of structures is used with flexibility: inverted structures ("Just as valuable is…"), a fronted participle clause ("Removed from familiar support systems…"), colons introducing explanation, and relative clauses. The great majority of sentences are error-free. The one slip is an agreement error with a compound subject — "Scholarships and part-time work reduces" should be *reduce* — which is the kind of very occasional error the Band 8 descriptor allows.

## Vocabulary
- **state-of-the-art facilities** — the most modern equipment and buildings available; strong for education and science topics.
- **open doors** — to create opportunities; idiomatic but acceptable in formal essays, e.g. *a degree that opens doors*.
- **well equipped** — having the skills needed for something; usually followed by *for* or *to*.
- **forge friendships** — to build strong friendships through effort; more precise than *make friends*.
- **culture shock** — disorientation on arriving in an unfamiliar culture; essential topic vocabulary.
- **brain drain** — the emigration of highly skilled people; useful for globalisation and migration questions.

## Next band
- Develop the brain-drain point fully from the home country's perspective — for instance, explain how governments could encourage graduates to return — so that every side raised is weighed with equal depth.
- Remove the agreement slip with the compound subject (scholarships and part-time work *reduce*); at Band 9 errors are rare and minor.
- Let the essay's structure carry more of the cohesion, reducing explicit signals such as "however" and "In short".
