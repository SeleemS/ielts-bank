## Prompt
As the population of many cities continues to grow, roads have become increasingly crowded and journeys that once took minutes can now take hours.

What problems does severe traffic congestion cause, and what measures could be taken to reduce it?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

## Essay
In many fast-growing cities, the daily journey to work has become one of the most frustrating parts of residents' lives. Severe congestion creates economic, environmental and social problems, but I believe it can be significantly reduced if governments ==invest in public transport== and make driving in the city centre more expensive.

The most obvious problem is lost time. When a commute that should take twenty minutes takes over an hour, employees arrive tired, businesses lose working hours and delivery costs rise for firms that rely on road transport. Across a whole city, this ==loss of productivity== becomes a serious drag on the economy. Congestion also harms public health. Vehicles that move slowly or stand still with their engines running release large amounts of exhaust fumes, and people who lives near main roads are more likely to suffer from breathing problems. In addition, long hours in traffic ==take a toll on== drivers' mental wellbeing, which can make them more agressive behind the wheel.

The most effective long-term solution is to offer a genuine ==alternative to the private car==. If buses are frequent and metro lines reach the suburbs, many commuters will happily leave their cars at home, since public transport is often cheaper and allows them to read or rest during the journey. However, better transport alone is rarely enough, because some drivers will continue to use their cars out of habit. For this reason, governments should also consider a ==congestion charge==. London introduced a daily fee for driving into its centre in 2003, and traffic in the charging zone fell noticeably in the following years. Finally, employers could allow ==staggered working hours== or some remote working, so that fewer people travel at the same time.

In conclusion, traffic congestion wastes time, damages health and slows economic activity. The best approach is to combine investment in public transport with measures that discourages unnecessary driving in city centres.

## Task Response
Both questions are addressed fully: the essay identifies economic, health and psychological problems and proposes three measures, and the position — that public transport investment and a congestion charge together are the answer — is clear from the introduction to the conclusion. Main ideas are extended and supported, for example the chain from a longer commute to lost working hours to a "drag on the economy", and the London example gives the charging argument real support. At times, though, the support over-generalises: "many commuters will happily leave their cars at home" is asserted rather than argued, and staggered hours are mentioned only briefly. Developing every idea to the same depth is what Band 8 requires.

## Coherence and Cohesion
Information is logically organised, with one body paragraph for problems and one for solutions, and each has a clear central topic. A range of cohesive devices is used appropriately, including contrast and reasoning ("However, better transport alone is rarely enough, because…", "For this reason") and clear reference ("this loss of productivity"). There is some over-use of additive signposts — "also", "In addition", "Finally" — and the problems paragraph moves between three separate issues quite quickly. Overall progression is clear throughout, which is solidly Band 7.

## Lexical Resource
There is a sufficient range of vocabulary for flexibility and precision, with well-chosen topic collocations such as "loss of productivity", "exhaust fumes", "congestion charge" and "staggered working hours", and an idiomatic "take a toll on". The writer shows awareness of style and collocation ("a serious drag on the economy", "behind the wheel"). There is an occasional spelling error — "agressive" should be *aggressive* — which is consistent with Band 7. To reach Band 8 the writer would need less common items used with more precision throughout, rather than concentrated in a few phrases.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy
A variety of complex structures is used: conditionals ("If buses are frequent…"), relative clauses, a purpose clause ("so that fewer people travel…") and a long subordinate time clause in the second paragraph. Many sentences are error-free, but there are two subject–verb agreement errors in relative clauses — "people who lives near main roads" (*live*) and "measures that discourages" (*discourage*). Good control with a few errors like these is the Band 7 descriptor; at Band 8 the majority of sentences must be completely accurate.

## Vocabulary
- **invest in public transport** — accurate verb + noun collocation for government spending; avoid *invest on*.
- **loss of productivity** — precise economic phrase for work time that is wasted; useful for any essay about commuting or health.
- **take a toll on** — to have a gradual harmful effect on something; idiomatic and natural in formal writing.
- **alternative to the private car** — formal way to refer to buses, trains and cycling without listing them.
- **congestion charge** — the specific name for a fee to drive into a busy area; more precise than *a tax on cars*.
- **staggered working hours** — start and finish times spread out across the day; a strong, specific solution phrase.

## Next band
- Argue each claim rather than asserting it — for instance, explain *why* some drivers keep driving "out of habit" and exactly how a charge changes that decision.
- Reduce reliance on "also", "In addition" and "Finally" by linking ideas through reference and sentence structure ("A less visible cost is…", "This is where pricing matters").
- Proofread relative clauses for agreement (people who *live*, measures that *discourage*) and check spelling of longer words such as *aggressive*.
