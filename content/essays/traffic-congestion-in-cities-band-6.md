## Prompt
As the population of many cities continues to grow, roads have become increasingly crowded and journeys that once took minutes can now take hours.

What problems does severe traffic congestion cause, and what measures could be taken to reduce it?

Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience. Write at least 250 words.

## Essay
Nowadays, many cities are growing very fast and the roads are becoming more and more crowded. Traffic jam is a big problem in big cities and people spend a lot of time in there cars. In this essay I will discuss the problems of traffic jam and some solutions for it.

Firstly, traffic jam cause many problems for people. The first problem is that people waste a lot of time. For example, in my city my father need almost two hours to get to work every day, although our home is not far from his office. When workers arrive late, the companys lose money and people feel very stress. The second problem is polution. Many cars stand on the road with the engine on and this make the air dirty. This is bad for people health, especially children and old people, because they breath this air every day.

Secondly, there are some solutions for this problem. The best solution is that the government should improve ==public transport==. If there are more buses and metro lines, people will ==leave their cars at home== and use the metro instead. For example, in my city a new metro line opened a few years ago and now many students use it to go to the university, especially in the ==rush hour==. Another solution is to make drivers ==pay a fee== when they drive in the city centre. I think this is good idea because people will think two times before they use their car. Also, companies can let workers ==work from home== some days a week.

In conclusion, traffic jam cause big problems like wasting time and pollution. The government must do something and the best way is to make public transport more better, so less people use cars in the city.

## Task Response
Both parts of the question are addressed: the essay names two problems (lost time and pollution) and three measures (public transport, a fee for driving in the centre, working from home), so it is relevant throughout. However, the parts are covered unevenly. Lost time is supported with a personal example, but the fee is justified in a single sentence and "work from home" is simply listed without any explanation of how it would reduce traffic. The conclusion repeats public transport and silently drops the other measures, so the overall position on what should be done is less clear than it could be — typical of Band 6, where some ideas are insufficiently developed.

## Coherence and Cohesion
The essay is coherent and there is a clear overall progression: introduction, one paragraph on problems, one on solutions, and a conclusion. The linking, however, is mechanical: "Firstly" opens the whole problems paragraph and "Secondly" the whole solutions paragraph, and inside them the writer relies on "The first problem… The second problem…" and "The best solution… Another solution… Also". Referencing is not always clear — "some solutions for this problem" follows a sentence about air pollution, so it is uncertain whether "this problem" means pollution or congestion as a whole. Cohesive devices work, but in the faulty, formulaic way the Band 6 descriptor describes.

## Lexical Resource
The vocabulary is only just adequate for the task, which places this criterion at Band 5. The range is limited and repetitive: "traffic jam" appears four times where *congestion* or *traffic volumes* would add variety, and "problem", "solution" and "big" ("a big problem in big cities", "big problems") recur throughout. There are noticeable spelling and word-formation errors — "there cars" (*their*), "companys" (*companies*), "polution" (*pollution*), "feel very stress" (*stressed*), "breath" (*breathe*) — and collocation slips such as "think two times" (*think twice*) and "Many cars stand on the road with the engine on" (*sit in traffic with their engines running*). Correct topic items like "public transport", "rush hour" and "pay a fee" show the basics are there; to reach Band 6 the writer needs fewer of these errors and at least some attempt at less common vocabulary (*exhaust fumes*, *commuters*, *congestion charge*).

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy
There is a genuine mix of simple and complex forms: a first conditional ("If there are more buses and metro lines, people will…"), a time clause ("When workers arrive late…") and a concessive clause ("although our home is not far from his office"). Errors are frequent, especially subject–verb agreement ("traffic jam cause", used twice, "my father need", "this make"), articles and countability ("Traffic jam is a big problem", "this is good idea"), the possessive ("people health" for *people's health*), a double comparative ("more better") and "less people" for *fewer people*. These errors are noticeable but rarely stop the reader understanding, which is the Band 6 level; for Band 7 more of the complex sentences must be error-free.

## Vocabulary
- **public transport** — the key topic term; at Band 7+ extend it to *a reliable public transport network* or *mass transit*.
- **leave their cars at home** — natural, idiomatic way to say people stop driving; a useful alternative to repeating *use their cars less*.
- **rush hour** — correct and precise; you can also write *during peak hours* or *at peak times*.
- **pay a fee** — clear and accurate; the specific policy name, *a congestion charge* or *road pricing*, would show more range.
- **work from home** — correct; upgrade to *remote working reduces the number of peak-time commuters* to explain the link to traffic.

## Next band
- Develop every measure you mention: explain *how* a driving fee changes behaviour and *why* home working takes cars off the road at peak times, and make the conclusion restate all of the measures, not just one.
- Replace "traffic jam" with a range of precise terms (*congestion*, *commuters*, *exhaust fumes*) and correct the spelling and word-form slips (their, companies, pollution, stressed, breathe); repeated lexical errors are what hold vocabulary at Band 5.
- Check every present-simple verb for agreement (congestion *causes*, my father *needs*, this *makes*) and remove "more better" and "less people"; fixing these patterns would lift grammar towards Band 7.
