## Prompt
You have seen an advertisement for volunteers to help at a local community centre and you would like to apply. Write a letter to the volunteer coordinator.

In your letter:

- say which volunteer role you are interested in and where you saw it advertised
- describe any relevant skills or experience you have
- explain when you would be available to help

Write at least 150 words. You do NOT need to write any addresses.

Begin your letter as follows: Dear Sir or Madam,

## Essay
Dear Sir or Madam,

==I am writing to apply for== the position of volunteer homework helper at your community centre, which I saw ==advertised on the noticeboard== at my local library last week.

I believe I have the skills and experience needed for this role. I am currently in the final year of a teaching degree, and as part of my course I have spent two placements in primary schools, where I supported small groups of children with reading and basic maths. In addition, for the past two years I have tutored my neighbour's son in English, and I have seen ==how much his confidence has grown==. I am patient, well-organised and I genuinely enjoy working with young people, especially those who ==find school work challenging==.

Regarding my availability, my university timetable is lighter this term, so I could help on Tuesday and Thursday afternoons from 3 to 6 p.m.. I would also be happy to cover the occasional Saturday morning if the centre needs extra helpers during exam periods.

I would be delighted to attend an interview at ==a time convenient for you==. Thank you for considering my application.

Yours faithfully,

Kwame Mensah

## Task Achievement
All three bullet points are covered and the purpose is clear from the opening sentence, which names both the role and where it was advertised. The tone is consistently formal and appropriate for an application ("I would be delighted to attend an interview"). Relevant experience is well chosen and supported with two examples, and availability is specific. The first bullet, though, is handled in a single sentence with no reason for choosing this particular role, and the availability does not mention when the writer could start — the bullets could be more fully extended, which keeps this at Band 7.

## Coherence and Cohesion
The letter is logically organised, with each paragraph built around one bullet point and a clear topic sentence ("I believe I have the skills and experience needed for this role", "Regarding my availability"). A range of cohesive devices is used appropriately, including relative clauses that link ideas ("where I supported small groups…", "especially those who…"). There is a slightly mechanical feel to "In addition" joining the two experience examples, but progression is clear throughout — Band 7.

## Lexical Resource
There is sufficient range to allow some flexibility and precision, with appropriate formal phrases ("advertised on the noticeboard", "a time convenient for you") and less common items used with awareness of collocation ("how much his confidence has grown", "find school work challenging"). There is one collocation error: "I have spent two placements" should be *completed two placements* (you spend *time*, not placements). With only occasional slips like this, Lexical Resource is Band 7.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy
A variety of complex structures is used with good control: present perfect for experience ("I have tutored"), non-defining and defining relative clauses, a conditional ("if the centre needs extra helpers") and polite modals ("I could help", "I would be delighted"). Most sentences are error-free. There are a few errors: the list "I am patient, well-organised and I genuinely enjoy…" is not parallel (*I am patient and well-organised, and I genuinely enjoy…*), and "6 p.m.." has a double full stop. Good control with a few errors fits Band 7.

## Vocabulary
- **I am writing to apply for** — the standard formal opening for an application letter.
- **advertised on the noticeboard** — the correct preposition and verb for where you saw a notice.
- **how much his confidence has grown** — shows the result of experience, not just the experience itself.
- **find school work challenging** — a tactful, precise way to describe struggling students.
- **a time convenient for you** — a polite formal phrase that leaves the choice to the reader.

## Next band
- Extend the first bullet with a reason for choosing this role (for example, your plan to become a teacher), and add a start date to the availability paragraph.
- Fix the small accuracy points: *completed two placements*, a parallel list (*patient and well-organised*), and a single full stop after *p.m.*
- Link the two experience examples more naturally than "In addition" — for instance, *Outside my studies, I have also…* — so the paragraph flows as one argument.
