On this page you get exactly that: the same Task 2 prompt answered at Band 6, 7 and 8, every version a genuine essay from our <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/studying-abroad-band-7">IELTS essay bank</a> and graded on all four criteria, plus an original model we wrote ourselves. Read one criterion straight down all three bands and the difference stops being abstract.</p>

  <h2>The two forms of the question (read the wording first)</h2>
  <p>Before any sample, settle the one decision that decides your Task Response score. "Advantages and disadvantages" questions arrive in two shapes, and they are <em>not</em> answered the same way.</p>
  <table>
    <thead>
      <tr><th>Prompt wording</th><th>What you must do</th><th>Do you give a verdict?</th></tr>
    </thead>
    <tbody>
      <tr><td><em>"What are the advantages and disadvantages of…?"</em></td><td>Explain both sides fairly and in roughly equal depth.</td><td><strong>No</strong> — a summary conclusion is enough.</td></tr>
      <tr><td><em>"Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"</em></td><td>Explain both sides <em>and</em> decide which is stronger, in the introduction and the conclusion.</td><td><strong>Yes</strong> — you must commit to a verdict.</td></tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
  <p>The most common mistake is treating the second form like the first: candidates describe both sides neatly, never say which wins, and cap their Task Response at Band 6 no matter how good the English is. If the word <em>outweigh</em> appears, you are writing an opinion essay in disguise. For the full structure, see our guide to the <a href="/blog/ielts-writing-task-2-advantages-disadvantages">advantages and disadvantages essay</a>.</p>

  <h2>A quick checklist for the "outweigh" form</h2>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>State your verdict in the introduction.</strong> "The advantages clearly outweigh the disadvantages" — decide and commit.</li>
    <li><strong>Give the stronger side more weight.</strong> Two developed advantages can earn one paragraph; the disadvantages another — but the balance should reflect your verdict.</li>
    <li><strong>Weigh, don't just list.</strong> Say <em>why</em> the benefits matter more: for example, because they last a career while the drawbacks are temporary.</li>
    <li><strong>Concede and answer.</strong> Raise the strongest drawback honestly, then show how it can be reduced.</li>
    <li><strong>Hold the verdict to the end.</strong> The conclusion restates the same position; it never goes neutral.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>The same prompt at Band 6, 7 and 8</h2>
  <p>Here is the "outweigh" prompt all three graded samples answer. Try it yourself on the <a href="/writingquestion/ielts-writing-task-2-studying-for-a-university-degree-abroad-wnck1o">studying-abroad practice page</a> before you read the models — writing first, reading second, is the order that actually raises your band.</p>
  <blockquote>
    <p><em>A growing number of students choose to leave their home country in order to complete a university degree in another part of the world. Do the advantages of studying for a degree abroad outweigh the disadvantages?</em></p>
  </blockquote>

  <h3><a href="/ielts-essay-bank/studying-abroad-band-6">Band 6</a> — both sides listed, never weighed</h3>
  <p>The Band 6 sample does the basics: it states a position ("the advantages are more than the disadvantages"), gives real advantages (education, language, independence) and real disadvantages (cost, loneliness), and reaches a conclusion. What holds it at 6 is visible in one sentence — <em>"I believe the advantages is bigger, because education and experience is important"</em>. The cost argument is raised and then never answered; the two sides are listed but never actually weighed against each other. Add the grammar slips ("the advantages is", "modern equipments", "adapt with"), and you have a textbook Band 6.</p>

  <h3><a href="/ielts-essay-bank/studying-abroad-band-7">Band 7</a> — extended reasons, weakened by word choice</h3>
  <p>The Band 7 version extends every reason: academic quality is tied to employability, personal growth is explained through specific responsibilities, and the disadvantages paragraph even adds the less obvious point of <em>brain drain</em>. The verdict ("clearly outweigh") is clear and consistent. What keeps it off Band 8 is lexical control, not ideas — "a big chance to become fluent" (for <em>a great opportunity</em>), "take responsibility of" (for <em>take responsibility for</em>), and "this can effect their performance" (confusing <em>effect</em> and <em>affect</em>). The grammar is mostly accurate with occasional agreement slips ("students usually pays"). Fix the word choice and this essay moves to 8.</p>

  <h3><a href="/ielts-essay-bank/studying-abroad-band-8">Band 8</a> — the drawbacks are answered, not just named</h3>
  <p>The Band 8 sample looks similar in structure, but one move separates it: it <strong>weighs</strong>. The disadvantages paragraph raises cost, culture shock and brain drain and then turns into a rebuttal — <em>"Yet most of these drawbacks can be mitigated"</em> — showing how scholarships, part-time work and university support reduce each one. The vocabulary is precise and natural ("state-of-the-art facilities", "navigate unfamiliar bureaucracy", "open doors"), and almost every sentence is error-free. That combination of a weighed argument and clean, flexible English is what Band 8 means. We break the method down further in <a href="/blog/ielts-task-2-band-6-vs-band-7-vs-band-8-same-essay">Band 6 vs 7 vs 8 on one prompt</a>.</p>

  <h2>An original model answer (our own writing, estimated Band 8)</h2>
  <p>To show the target on a different topic, here is an <strong>original</strong> answer we wrote for an "outweigh" prompt on online study. The band is our own honest estimate, not an official mark — we do not claim examiner marking, only examiner-style criteria.</p>
  <blockquote>
    <p><em>Prompt: The number of university courses delivered entirely through online learning platforms has grown rapidly. Do the advantages of this trend outweigh the disadvantages?</em></p>
    <p>The expansion of fully online degrees has divided opinion, with some fearing a decline in educational quality. While these concerns are legitimate, I believe the advantages of online learning platforms outweigh their drawbacks for most students.</p>
    <p>The strongest advantage is access. A learner in a remote town, or one holding down a full-time job, can now study with universities that would once have been closed to them by distance or cost, and can do so at hours that fit around work and family. Because recorded lectures can be paused and revisited, students who need more time to absorb difficult material are no longer left behind by the pace of a lecture hall — a flexibility that traditional campuses struggle to match.</p>
    <p>The clearest disadvantage is the loss of face-to-face contact, which can weaken both motivation and the informal learning that happens between peers. Yet this drawback is shrinking rather than fixed: well-designed platforms now build in live seminars, discussion forums and group projects that recreate much of that interaction, and students who actively use these tools report little sense of isolation. The concern is real, but it is increasingly answerable through design.</p>
    <p>On balance, then, the gains in access and flexibility reach far more people than the drawbacks disadvantage, and those drawbacks can be reduced. For the committed student, online platforms open a door that was previously shut, which is why I consider their advantages the stronger side.</p>
  </blockquote>
  <p>Notice the Band 8 moves from the checklist: a verdict in the introduction, one extended advantage, a drawback that is conceded <em>and</em> answered, and a conclusion that restates the same position.   <h2>The three mistakes that cap these essays</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Listing without weighing.</strong> Two neat paragraphs that never say which side is stronger answer the wrong question when the prompt asks "outweigh". Decide, and defend the decision.</li>
    <li><strong>A neutral conclusion.</strong> Ending with "there are both advantages and disadvantages" throws away the verdict you set up. Restate your position.</li>
    <li><strong>Describing a drawback but not answering it.</strong> The jump from Band 7 to Band 8 is conceding the strongest disadvantage and then showing how it can be reduced — exactly what the Band 8 sample does.</li>
  </ol>
