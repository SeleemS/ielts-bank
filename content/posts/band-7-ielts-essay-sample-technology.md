  <h2>The prompt</h2>
  <p>This is a <strong>positive-or-negative development</strong> question on a technology theme — one of the most common Task 2 frames:</p>
  <blockquote>
    <p><em>Robots and automated machines are carrying out an increasing share of the work in factories that was once done by human hands. Is this increasing automation of manufacturing a positive or negative development?</em></p>
  </blockquote>

  <h2>The Band 7 sample answer</h2>
  <p>Here is the full response, quoted from the essay bank. Read it once for the argument, then read the analysis that follows.</p>
  <p>The essay opens by framing the debate and stating a clear position: automation is, <em>"on balance, a positive development."</em> It then builds three body paragraphs — productivity, safety, and the human cost of job losses — before a short conclusion. Two ideas do most of the work:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Productivity.</strong> Machines <em>"can operate around the clock without breaks"</em> and work with a precision humans cannot maintain over a long shift, so goods cost less to make.</li>
    <li><strong>Safety.</strong> Robots take over dangerous tasks — <em>"handling toxic chemicals, operating heavy presses or working in extreme heat"</em> — leaving staff in safer, more skilled supervisory roles.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Crucially, the essay does not ignore the downside. It concedes that displaced workers, <em>"particularly older employees with few qualifications, often struggle to find new employment,"</em> then answers that objection with a solution: government and employer investment in <em>"retraining schemes"</em> so workers can move into maintenance, programming or logistics. You can read the complete text on its <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/automation-of-factory-work-band-7">essay bank page</a>.</p>

  <h2>Why it scores Band 7 — criterion by criterion</h2>

  <h3>Task Response: 7</h3>
  <p>The question is answered directly and the position is clear in the introduction and maintained to the end. Each main idea is extended and supported rather than just listed, and the negative side is genuinely addressed and then answered — that give-and-take is a Band 7 habit. What holds it <em>at</em> 7 is that some support over-generalises: the claim that savings <em>"are often passed on to consumers"</em> is asserted rather than explained. A Band 8 answer would give the mechanism (competition between manufacturers forces prices down).</p>

  <h3>Coherence &amp; Cohesion: 7</h3>
  <p>Every body paragraph has one central topic — productivity, safety, job losses — and a range of cohesive devices is used appropriately: <em>"As a result," "for instance,"</em> and the reference <em>"this problem."</em> The fourth paragraph turns from the objection to the writer's response with <em>"However."</em> There is still some reliance on familiar signposts (<em>"On the other hand," "In conclusion"</em>), which is exactly why it is a 7 and not an 8, but the progression is always clear.</p>

  <h3>Lexical Resource: 7</h3>
  <p>The vocabulary is where a lot of Band 7 candidates and Band 6 candidates diverge, so this is worth studying closely. The essay uses precise topic collocations — <em>"a rise in productivity," "retraining schemes," "displaced workers"</em> — and the natural idiom <em>"around the clock."</em> These are ordinary words used accurately, not rare words dropped in to impress. The slips that keep it at 7 are word-form and spelling errors: <em>"a big lost of income"</em> should be <em>a large loss of income</em>, and <em>"who's jobs"</em> should be <em>whose jobs</em>. For more topic language of this kind, see our <a href="/blog/ielts-vocabulary-technology">technology vocabulary list</a>.</p>

  <h3>Grammatical Range &amp; Accuracy: 7</h3>
  <p>There is a real variety of complex structures used with control: a concessive opening (<em>"Although this trend…"</em>), non-defining relative clauses, a conditional (<em>"If governments and employers invest…"</em>), and a contrast built with <em>"not caused by the technology itself but by a lack of support."</em> Most sentences are error-free. A few agreement slips remain — <em>"Electronic devices and household appliances … has become"</em> (should be <em>have become</em>) and <em>"logistics, which is growing sectors"</em> (should be <em>which are</em>) — and at Band 8 the great majority of sentences must be completely clean.</p>
