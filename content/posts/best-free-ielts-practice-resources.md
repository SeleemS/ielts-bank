  <p>You do not need to spend money to prepare well for IELTS. Between the test's own partners and a handful of long-running teacher sites, there is enough free material to take you from first practice test to exam day. What the free ecosystem lacks is not volume but honesty about <em>fit</em>: every resource is best at one thing and weak at another. This list ranks the best free IELTS resources for 2026, official sources first, with one genuine limitation for each — including for our own tools, which appear at the end and are clearly marked as ours.</p>

  <h2>1. Official IELTS sample questions (ielts.org)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> seeing exactly what the real test asks. The official IELTS website at <a href="https://ielts.org">ielts.org</a> publishes free sample questions for every section and both modules — Academic and General Training — straight from the people who set the exam, so the formats, instructions and difficulty are exactly right.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> the sample sets are small. They are ideal for learning what each question type looks like, but there is nowhere near enough volume for weeks of practice, and nothing marks your answers for you.</p>

  <h2>2. British Council free practice tests (takeielts.britishcouncil.org)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> free full practice tests from a test partner. The British Council's Take IELTS site offers free practice for all four sections, in both paper and computer-delivered formats, so it is the closest free simulation of the official computer test interface.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> Writing and Speaking practice comes without scoring — you get the questions and sample responses, but no feedback on your own answers unless you find a teacher.</p>

  <h2>3. IDP IELTS preparation hub (ielts.idp.com)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> structured preparation guidance from the other co-owner of the test. IDP's prep hub collects free sample questions, preparation webinars, and section-by-section guides, and its explanations of test rules (word limits, timing, band descriptors) are authoritative.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> the material is spread across many pages and some resources ask you to register with an email address before you can access them.</p>

  <h2>4. Cambridge free activities and sample papers (cambridgeenglish.org)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> authentic exam-style tasks from the organisation that writes the papers. Cambridge English publishes free IELTS preparation activities and sample tasks, and Cambridge University Press &amp; Assessment's numbered <em>Cambridge IELTS</em> books are the gold standard for authentic past-style tests.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> the genuinely free portion is a taster — the full Cambridge IELTS practice-test books themselves are paid products, so budget for one or two if you want their complete tests.</p>

  <h2>5. IELTS Liz (ieltsliz.com)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> free lessons, tips and model answers from an experienced IELTS teacher. IELTS Liz has been running for over a decade and offers hundreds of free lessons, model answers and topic lists, with clear advice aimed at candidates targeting bands 7 to 9. It is one of the most consistently recommended teacher sites, and everything essential is free.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> it is a guidance site, not a practice platform — there are no interactive, auto-scored tests, so you still need somewhere to actually drill questions under timed conditions.</p>

  <h2>6. Our practice question bank (this site — free)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> volume drilling with instant scoring. Full disclosure: this is our own resource. The IELTS-Bank question banks for <a href="/readingquestion">Reading</a>, <a href="/listeningquestion">Listening</a>, <a href="/writingquestion">Writing</a> and <a href="/speakingquestion">Speaking</a> are free, timed, auto-scored where the real test is auto-scored, and organised by question type, with <a href="/mock-test">full mock tests</a> when you want the complete experience.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> our questions are original practice material written to mirror the official format — they are not official past papers, so pair them with the official samples above to calibrate against the real thing.</p>

  <h2>7. Our band estimator and band calculator (this site — free)</h2>
  <p><strong>Best for:</strong> knowing where you stand. Also ours: the <a href="/band-estimator">15-minute band estimator</a> gives you a four-skill starting range from real practice questions, and the <a href="/band-calculator">band score calculator</a> converts Listening and Reading raw scores and applies the official rounding rule to estimate your overall band.</p>
  <p><strong>Limitation:</strong> both produce estimates. Raw-to-band conversions vary slightly between real test versions, and no short estimator can be as reliable as a full, examiner-marked test.</p>

One sample cannot establish recurring errors or measure improvement. Check each suggestion against your actual text.</p>

  <h2>How to combine them</h2>
  <ol>
    <li>Start with the <strong>official samples</strong> (ielts.org, British Council, IDP) to learn the formats.</li>
    <li>Take our <a href="/band-estimator">band estimator</a> or a full practice test to find your baseline.</li>
    <li>Drill your weakest question types in volume — free banks like ours exist for exactly this.</li>
    <li>Use teacher sites like IELTS Liz for strategy on the question types that resist drilling.</li>
    <li>Finish with one or two authentic Cambridge tests under strict exam timing before test day.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>No single free resource covers everything — the official sites have authenticity but little volume and no feedback; teacher sites have strategy but no practice engine; we have volume and scoring but original rather than official questions. Used together, they cover each other's gaps, and none of them costs anything to start. For what the test itself looks like section by section, see our <a href="/ielts-test-format">IELTS test format guide</a>.</p>
