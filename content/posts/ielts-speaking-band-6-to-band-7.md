  <p>Plenty of candidates sit the <strong>IELTS Speaking</strong> test, communicate everything they want to say, and still come away with Band 6. The frustrating part is that Band 7 is not about speaking flawless English — it is about speaking more <em>smoothly</em>, with a wider range, and with fewer errors in the harder sentences. This guide shows exactly what the examiner is listening for and the habits that move you up a band.</p>

  <h2>How IELTS Speaking Is Scored</h2>
  <p>The Speaking test lasts about <strong>11 to 14 minutes</strong> and has three parts: a short interview (Part 1), a long-turn cue card with one minute to prepare (Part 2), and a two-way discussion (Part 3). Your performance is marked on <strong>four equally weighted criteria</strong>, each worth 25%:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Fluency and Coherence</strong> — how smoothly and logically you speak.</li>
    <li><strong>Lexical Resource</strong> — the range and precision of your vocabulary.</li>
    <li><strong>Grammatical Range and Accuracy</strong> — the variety and correctness of your structures.</li>
    <li><strong>Pronunciation</strong> — how clearly and naturally you are understood.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>The four scores are averaged and reported to the nearest half band, so lifting even one criterion from a 6 to a 7 can change your result. It is worth learning each one in detail on our guide to the <a href="/blog/ielts-speaking-band-descriptors">IELTS Speaking band descriptors</a>.</p>

  <h2>Fluency: Speak at Length Without Effort</h2>
  <p>This is where most Band 6 marks are lost. At Band 6 you are "willing to speak at length" but coherence slips because of <strong>repetition, self-correction and hesitation</strong>. At Band 7 you speak "without noticeable effort," and any hesitation is to search for ideas rather than for basic words.</p>
  <p>The fix is not to speak faster — it is to stop stalling on small words:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Never repair your grammar out loud.</strong> If you start a sentence wrong, keep going. Constant self-correction ("I go — I mean, I went") is what pins candidates to Band 6.</li>
    <li><strong>Use natural fillers to buy thinking time</strong> — "That's an interesting question," "I'd say," "Off the top of my head." These sound fluent; "um... er... how to say" does not.</li>
    <li><strong>Extend every answer with a reason and an example.</strong> One-sentence answers give the examiner nothing to score.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Our guide to <a href="/blog/ielts-speaking-fluency-and-coherence">fluency and coherence</a> has drills for exactly this.</p>

  <h2>Vocabulary: Show Range, Not Just Correctness</h2>
  <p>Band 6 vocabulary makes meaning clear "in spite of inappropriacies." Band 7 uses "some less common and idiomatic vocabulary" and shows "awareness of collocation and style." You do not need rare words — you need precise ones and natural pairings.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Learn collocations, not single words.</strong> Say "heavy traffic," "a close friend," "a major concern" — not "big traffic" or "strong friend."</li>
    <li><strong>Add one or two idiomatic phrases per answer</strong> — "it's a mixed blessing," "to be honest," "at the end of the day" — used naturally, not forced in.</li>
    <li><strong>Paraphrase the question</strong> instead of echoing its words, which also shows lexical range.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Grammar: Get the Complex Sentences Right</h2>
  <p>At Band 6 you mix simple and complex structures "but with limited flexibility" and make "frequent mistakes with complex structures." Band 7 uses "a range of complex structures with some flexibility" and "frequently produces error-free sentences." The target is not zero errors — it is more clean complex sentences.</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Use conditionals and relative clauses</strong>: "If I had more time, I would…"; "the city where I grew up…"</li>
    <li><strong>Vary your tenses deliberately</strong> — Part 1 often invites the past ("What did you enjoy as a child?") and Part 3 the future or hypothetical.</li>
    <li><strong>Master the errors that recur most</strong>, especially subject–verb agreement and article use, so your complex sentences stay clean.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Pronunciation: Be Easy to Understand</h2>
  <p>Band 7 pronunciation shows "all the positive features of Band 6 and some of Band 8." In practice, be understood without strain: use <strong>word stress, sentence stress and intonation</strong> so your meaning is clear. You are not marked on having a British or American accent — only on clarity and natural rhythm.</p>

  <h2>A Simple Plan to Close the Gap</h2>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Record yourself</strong> answering real questions and count your fillers, self-corrections and repeated words.</li>
    <li><strong>Rebuild weak answers</strong> with a reason, an example, and one collocation or idiom.</li>
    <li><strong>Drill one complex structure a day</strong> until it comes out automatically under pressure.</li>
    <li><strong>Practise full timed tests</strong> on our <a href="/speakingquestion">IELTS Speaking practice hub</a> so the format feels routine on test day.</li>
  </ol>
  <p>Band 7 in Speaking rewards smoothness and range over perfection. Stop repairing every sentence, widen your vocabulary with natural pairings, land your complex structures cleanly, and speak so you are effortless to follow — and the half bands you need will follow with them.</p>
