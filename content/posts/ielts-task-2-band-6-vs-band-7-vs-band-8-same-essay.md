  <p>The most useful way to understand IELTS Writing bands is not to read the descriptors in the abstract — it is to see <strong>one prompt answered at three different levels</strong> and watch what actually changes. So that is what this article does. We take a single Task 2 question from our <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/free-university-education-band-7">IELTS essay bank</a> and walk through the Band 6, Band 7 and Band 8 responses to it, side by side, so the jump from one score to the next stops being mysterious.</p>

  <p>The prompt is the same for all three: <em>"In some countries, students must pay high tuition fees to attend university… To what extent do you agree or disagree that university education should be free for all?"</em> You can write your own answer to it first on the <a href="/writingquestion/ielts-writing-task-2-should-university-education-be-free-for-everyone-p5ttxn">free tuition practice page</a>, then read on to place yourself.</p>

  <h2>The one-page version: what changes from 6 to 7 to 8</h2>

  <p>Notice what is <em>not</em> on that list: length, and "advanced" vocabulary. The Band 8 sample is barely longer than the Band 6 one. The score rises because of control, precision and development — not word count.</p>

  <h2>Band 6: competent, but under-developed</h2>
  <p>The <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/free-university-education-band-6">Band 6 answer</a> is not broken. It opens clearly — <em>"Nowadays, university education is very expensive in many countries and a lot of students can not pay the fees"</em> — states a position ("I agree"), and holds it to the conclusion. That clear, maintained position is exactly why it earns 6 rather than something lower.</p>
  <p>What holds it <em>at</em> 6 is visible in three places:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Thin development.</strong> The funding counter-argument is dismissed in a single line ("take this money from other things, for example the military"), and the conclusion introduces a brand-new idea ("check that students are serious") without explaining it.</li>
    <li><strong>Mechanical cohesion.</strong> Every body paragraph begins with a stock connector, and references such as "this problem" leave the reader guessing what is meant.</li>
    <li><strong>Frequent grammar slips.</strong> Subject-verb agreement fails repeatedly: <em>"free education give,"</em> <em>"their family don't,"</em> <em>"the government need."</em> None of these stop communication — which is the Band 6 boundary — but they are constant.</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Band 7: the same argument, brought under control</h2>
  <p>The <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/free-university-education-band-7">Band 7 answer</a> makes the same core points but controls them. The introduction reframes the issue instead of restating it: <em>"the cost of a university degree has risen so sharply that tuition fees now decide who can study, rather than ability alone."</em> That is not fancier vocabulary — it is a sharper idea.</p>
  <p>Three things change from Band 6:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Ideas are extended.</strong> The access argument names a consequence ("take on large debts which affects their lives for decades") and the essay uses a real example — Germany abolishing tuition — instead of a vague gesture.</li>
    <li><strong>Cohesion is varied.</strong> Linkers such as "In this sense" and "Admittedly" show relationships between ideas rather than just numbering them.</li>
    <li><strong>Accuracy improves.</strong> A variety of complex structures — a "provided that" conditional, non-defining relative clauses — appear, and most sentences are now error-free. The few remaining slips ("take their studies serious," "it's talent") are what keep it off Band 8.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>This is the single most valuable comparison for most candidates, because the Band 6-to-7 wall is where scores stall. If that is you, our dedicated guide on <a href="/blog/ielts-writing-band-6-to-band-7">how to go from Band 6 to Band 7 in writing</a> breaks the same four gaps down into drills.</p>

  <h2>Band 8: precision and near-perfect accuracy</h2>
  <p>The <a href="/ielts-essay-bank/free-university-education-band-8">Band 8 answer</a> reads differently from the first word: <em>"Whether a university education should cost students anything at all is one of the more divisive questions in education policy."</em> The argument is then made with unusual precision — fees <em>"ration education by wealth rather than by ability,"</em> and the state <em>"recoups its investment many times over across a working life."</em></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The counter-argument is defeated, not just noted.</strong> The essay concedes free tuition subsidises the middle class, then answers with three specific policy fixes (capped funded places, progress conditions, targeted maintenance grants).</li>
    <li><strong>Vocabulary is precise, not rare.</strong> "Untapped potential," "financial deterrent," "a public good" — ordinary words placed exactly. Nothing looks memorised.</li>
    <li><strong>Grammar is almost flawless.</strong> Fronted clauses, participle phrases and a colon used correctly, with essentially one slip in the whole essay ("The Nordic countries… demonstrates"). That near error-free control is the heart of Band 8.</li>
  </ul>
