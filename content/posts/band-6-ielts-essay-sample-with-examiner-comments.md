  <h2>The prompt</h2>
  <p>This is an <strong>advantages-and-disadvantages</strong> question on an education theme — one of the most common Task 2 frames:</p>
  <blockquote>
    <p><em>A growing number of students choose to leave their home country in order to complete a university degree in another part of the world. Do the advantages of studying for a degree abroad outweigh the disadvantages?</em></p>

  <h2>The Band 6 sample answer</h2>
  <p>Here is the full response, quoted from the essay bank. Read it once for the argument, then read the criterion-by-criterion analysis that follows.</p>
  <blockquote>
    <p>Today, more and more students go to other countries to study in university. They think it is better for their future. In my opinion, the advantages of studying abroad are more than the disadvantages, although there are some problems.</p>
    <p>The first advantage is that students can get a better quality of education. Many universities in countries like UK, USA and Australia are famous in the world and they have modern equipments and good teachers. A degree from this universities can help students to find a good job when they come back. The second advantage is that students can improve their English or other language because they use it every day. Also, they become more independent, because they must cook, clean and manage their money by themself. For example, my cousin studied in Canada for four years and when he returned he was more confident and mature.</p>
    <p>However, there are also disadvantages. The biggest problem is the cost. The tuition fees and living costs are very high and many families must borrow money to pay them. Another problem is that students can feel homesick and alone in a new culture, especially in the first year. Some students cannot adapt with the new culture and their marks become low. For example, a friend of mine returned home after only one year because he could not make friends and he missed his family too much.</p>
    <p>To sum up, studying abroad has disadvantages like the cost and the loneliness, but I believe the advantages is bigger, because education and experience is important for the future. Students should prepare good before they go.</p>
  </blockquote>

  <h2>Examiner-style comments, criterion by criterion</h2>

  <h3>Task Response: 6</h3>
  <p>The essay answers the question directly, and the position — that the advantages are greater — is stated in the introduction and repeated in the conclusion. Relevant advantages (quality of education, language, independence) and disadvantages (cost, loneliness) are all presented, so every part of the task is addressed. What keeps it at 6 is that it never actually <em>weighs</em> the two sides: the cost argument is raised and then left unanswered, and the conclusion simply asserts that "education and experience is important" rather than explaining why those benefits beat the drawbacks. The final sentence — "Students should prepare good before they go" — introduces a brand-new idea with no development, a classic Band 6 conclusion.</p>

  <h3>Coherence &amp; Cohesion: 6</h3>
  <p>Organisation is clear: introduction, advantages, disadvantages, conclusion, with an obvious overall progression. The problem is that the cohesion is <em>mechanical</em>. Every point is flagged with a stock signpost — "The first advantage", "The second advantage", "Also", "However", "The biggest problem", "Another problem", "To sum up" — rather than with linking that shows how ideas relate. Referencing wobbles too: in the second sentence, "They think it is better" never makes clear what studying abroad is better <em>than</em>. The cohesive devices work, but in the formulaic, sometimes faulty way the Band 6 descriptor describes.</p>

  <h3>Lexical Resource: 6</h3>
  <p>The vocabulary is adequate for the task, with correct topic phrases such as "living costs", "feel homesick" and "manage their money", and there is some attempt at less common language. But the attempts are often inaccurate: "the advantages … are more than the disadvantages" should be <em>outweigh</em>, "adapt with" should be <em>adapt to</em>, and "their marks become low" is an unnatural collocation (<em>their grades suffer</em>). There is also visible repetition of "advantage", "problem" and "culture". None of this blocks meaning, which is what keeps the criterion at 6 rather than dropping to 5.</p>

  <h3>Grammatical Range &amp; Accuracy: 6</h3>
  <p>There is a genuine mix of simple and complex sentences, including concessive and reason clauses ("although there are some problems", "because they use it every day") and a time clause ("when he returned"). But errors are frequent and spread across several patterns: articles ("countries like UK, USA" for <em>the UK, the USA</em>; "study in university" for <em>at university</em>), countability ("modern equipments"), determiners ("this universities" for <em>these</em>), a reflexive pronoun ("by themself"), subject–verb agreement ("the advantages is", "education and experience is") and adjective–adverb confusion ("prepare good" for <em>well</em>). They are noticeable throughout but rarely reduce communication — the exact Band 6 boundary.</p>

  <h2>The pattern behind all four scores</h2>
  <p>Read those four comments together and the same story repeats: the essay is <strong>relevant and clear, but thin and error-prone</strong>. That is Band 6 in one line. It is not a broken essay — it is a competent one that never fully develops its ideas or brings its sentences under control. Fixing Band 6 is therefore not about writing more or reaching for rare words; it is about extending each point and cleaning up recurring errors.</p>

  <h2>What would lift this essay to Band 7</h2>
  <p>Here is what a Band 7 version of the very same essay would change — and none of it is "harder vocabulary":</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Weigh the sides explicitly.</strong> Say <em>why</em> a better degree and greater independence are worth the cost and the loneliness — for example, because the benefits last a whole career while the difficulties are mostly temporary. That single move upgrades Task Response.</li>
    <li><strong>Vary the cohesion.</strong> Replace "The first advantage / The second advantage" with linking that shows relationships ("More importantly", "In practical terms", "Admittedly"), and make every reference word point clearly to something.</li>
    <li><strong>Use precise verbs and correct collocations.</strong> <em>Outweigh</em> instead of "are more than", <em>adapt to a new culture</em>, <em>their grades suffer</em> — accurate ordinary language, not showy words.</li>
    <li><strong>Kill the repeated grammar errors.</strong> Articles before country names (<em>the UK</em>), plural determiners (<em>these universities</em>), uncountable nouns (<em>equipment</em>), agreement (<em>the advantages are</em>) and adverbs after verbs (<em>prepare well</em>).</li>
  </ol>
